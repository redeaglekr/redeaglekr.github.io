Looking up,
to see deeper

Everything we see in the Universe ultimately originates from stars. That alone makes them interesting, beyond simply enjoying their beauty in the night sky. For me, this fascination is what makes astrophysics so exciting: our ability to capture the light from distant objects, understand how they form and evolve, and piece together the story of our own origin. Thus, the primary objective of my research is to understand how stars and planetary systems evolve, how their evolution manifests in observations, and how these systems contribute to the physical and chemical evolution of our Galaxy.

About me

I'm an astrophysicist with a PhD in Physics from the University of Sydney. My research focuses on understanding stars, their evolution and the history of our Galaxy. I work primarily with asteroseismology and large-scale astronomical datasets from space missions such as Kepler and TESS.

Research

My research spans observational astronomy, from the detection and characterisation of exoplanets to the study of stellar oscillations and the application of asteroseismology to Galactic archaeology. I work with large photometric and spectroscopic datasets to investigate the properties, structure and evolution of stars.
